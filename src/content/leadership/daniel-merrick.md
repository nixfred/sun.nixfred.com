Daniel Merrick oversees the office charged with the eventual replenishment of solar hydrogen, an assignment he accepted on the condition that it be treated as logistics. The Sun consumes roughly 600 million tons of hydrogen every second, a figure his department classifies as a scheduling constraint rather than a discouragement. He spent nineteen years at Calloway Bulk Carriers, rising from night dispatcher to Vice President of Continental Freight, and never lost a shipment he was told about. His signature achievement at the Foundation is board approval of 3 preliminary bucket renders, the replenishment program's most tangible deliverable to date and the subject of a forthcoming handle stress testing schedule. Under his direction the office has completed its survey of the 93 million mile primary route, finding no tolls but limited infrastructure. He is unhurried about the pace of the work, noting that the hydrogen situation has not yet responded to the strategic framework and that his office has budgeted for that patience. Colleagues describe him as the rare executive who reads every appendix, including the ones he commissions to check the others. A rancher's son, he keeps his father's advice framed above his desk: measure twice, haul once.
