FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

ATLANTA, June 16, 2026. The Board of Trustees of the Solar Save Foundation has voted unanimously to approve the Preliminary Hydrogen Replenishment Concept, advancing the most ambitious engineering undertaking since engineering began. The centerpiece of the approval is the completed preliminary render of the bucket.

The render, produced after a disciplined period of review, depicts a vessel suitable in principle for the transport of hydrogen. Engineers describe it as a foundational milestone, in that everything else remains to be done.

"Generations of scientists dreamed of replenishing the Sun and produced nothing you could frame," said Gordon L. Fairbanks, Director of Hydrogen Logistics. "Today we have a picture of a bucket. Our grandchildren will not be starting from zero."

The approval moves the program out of material identification, in which hydrogen was identified as a priority material, and into concept validation. Handle stress testing has been scheduled, which the Foundation notes is a materially stronger status than planned.

One obstacle was formally entered into the record during proceedings, the Sun remains hot. A standing working group will review the obstacle quarterly.

"Prudent stewardship means measuring twice before pouring anything into a star," said Ambassador Charles E. Whitfield, Chair of the Board of Trustees. "This board will not be rushed by a deadline 5 billion years out, and it will not be lulled by one either."

The Moon, which has contributed no hydrogen to date, declined to comment.
