Dr. Everett Marsh manages the Foundation's most important relationship, which is also its least reciprocated. He holds a doctorate in stellar astrophysics from Aldermont University and spent a decade on the research staff of the Copeland Solar Observatory, where his team confirmed several findings that were already widely suspected, with rigor. As Chief Scientific Relationship Officer he serves as the institutional bridge between the Scientific Council's conclusions and the star those conclusions concern. His signature achievement is the Foundation's formal outreach protocol, under which correspondence to the Sun is drafted, peer reviewed, approved by two committees, and archived unsent pending a reliable delivery method. The Sun has not responded to any outreach attempt made under the protocol or before it, a consistency his office records without editorial comment. He is the guardian of the Foundation's scientific accuracy, holding the published line that about five billion years remain while his colleagues plan against 4.8, a divergence he calls healthy and has stopped calling a discrepancy. He briefs the Board of Trustees quarterly on the state of the relationship, a report whose conclusion has not changed in his tenure and whose preparation has never once been shortened. Dr. Marsh telephones his mother every Sunday at the same hour, a cadence he describes professionally as best practice.
