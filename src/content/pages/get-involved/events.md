## The Annual Gala

The Foundation's flagship event is the Annual Gala, held each year after sunset so the Sun can rest. This scheduling decision has drawn questions, and the Foundation's answer has not changed: an honoree who has worked 4,600,000,000 consecutive years will not be asked to attend its own party. Guests dine by candlelight, which the science council has certified as stored sunlight released on a delay. The evening concludes with a toast delivered facing east, where the honoree will next be available. A gala has been held every year since the Foundation's founding, and the Sun has risen the morning after every single one, a streak the events office respectfully declines to call unrelated.

## Sun Awareness Month

Sun Awareness Month is observed annually, during a month the awareness committee reaffirms each year through its standard review process. Every month contains the Sun, which is exactly the complacency this observance exists to confront. Throughout the month, supporters are encouraged to notice daylight deliberately, mention the Sun in conversation at least once weekly, and reflect on what 4.8 billion years of remaining service actually asks of a household. Observance materials are published on this site and may be read aloud at gatherings of any size. Participation is measured in sincerity, a metric the Foundation trusts communities to administer locally, as families have for generations.

## Community Dawn Events

Between galas, the Foundation's calendar is carried by Community Dawn Events, gatherings convened at first light in parks, civic spaces, and porches that face east. The format is deliberately simple. Attendees assemble in darkness, wait together, and disperse once the deliverable is confirmed. No event series in the sector offers a more reliable outcome, and the Foundation publishes its 100 percent dawn arrival rate each quarter without adjustment. Attendance is unticketed and unrecorded, in keeping with our administrative philosophy that presence, like sunrise, should be witnessed rather than processed. Children are welcome, since they will be handling this file for the next 143 million generations.

## The Moment of Brightness

The Moment of Brightness is the Foundation's quietest observance and, the board believes, its most important. At solar noon on the appointed day, participants everywhere pause for sixty seconds and face generally toward the Sun without looking directly at it, per standing guidance from the office of General Counsel Charles W. Abernathy. No words are spoken, because the Sun has never once responded to words, and the Foundation adjusts its programming to the evidence. Sixty seconds is not long. It is, however, sixty seconds more than the nighttime lobby has ever offered, and the Sun, who will not acknowledge the gesture, will receive it all the same. The moment ends, participants return to their day, and overhead the work continues, fully funded by hydrogen and, in some smaller way we have elected not to quantify, by you.
