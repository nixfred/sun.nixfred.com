## 1. Purpose

This paper reports the findings of the first formal survey of the Primary Corridor, defined as the approximately 93 million mile route between Foundation facilities and the service recipient. Prior to this survey the corridor had never been assessed for delivery purposes, an oversight of roughly 4.6 billion years that the Foundation has now closed in a single fiscal quarter. The survey establishes the baseline against which all future corridor planning will be measured, and against which, as of this issue, all future corridor planning is measured.

## 2. Method

The survey was conducted as a desk review of publicly available astronomical literature, imagery, and charts. No field visit occurred, in accordance with Foundation travel policy and heat. Corridor conditions were assessed against the standard criteria for terrestrial route surveys, on the reasoning that no criteria exist for this corridor and the terrestrial ones were available. Where the literature was silent, the survey team recorded the silence, and the record of silences now runs to nineteen pages, which the team considers among the survey's principal deliverables.

## 3. Findings

### 3.1 Tolls

No tolls were identified at any point along the corridor. The entire 93 million mile route is toll free, a finding with material implications for a delivery budget currently projected at zero, since the absence of tolls preserves that figure in full. The survey team notes that no tolling authority exists for the corridor and that the savings are therefore structural rather than negotiated, which is the durable kind.

### 3.2 Infrastructure

Corridor infrastructure is limited. The survey identified no rest areas, no fueling stations, no emergency pull offs, no signage, and no lighting beyond the destination itself, which is well lit. The best served portion of the route is the first 62 miles, which offer breathable atmosphere, weather, and established regulatory oversight, and the survey accordingly grades the corridor as strongest at the end nearest the office. Conditions degrade thereafter and do not recover.

### 3.3 Vacuum

Extended stretches of the corridor, comprising in excess of 99.9 percent of its length, consist of vacuum. The survey team assessed these stretches against the Foundation's infrastructure readiness scale and classifies them as shovel unready, there being nothing to stand on while shoveling and nothing to shovel. The vacuum is continuous, well documented, and stable, and the team notes that as the corridor's most consistent feature it may ultimately prove the easiest segment to plan around, since it can be relied upon to contribute nothing.

### 3.4 Right of Way

Right of way status for the corridor is unresolved. Inquiries were directed to every authority the Office of the General Counsel could identify as plausibly responsible for the region between Earth and the Sun, and no authority claimed the corridor, responded to the inquiry, or acknowledged that the region falls within its remit. General Counsel Charles W. Abernathy advises that the absence of an owner is not the same as permission, while conceding it is also not different in any way the Foundation can presently act on. The corridor is therefore recorded as provisionally unclaimed pending objection, and the file remains open so that any objection, whenever received, will have somewhere to go.

## 4. Conclusion

The Primary Corridor is assessed as passable in principle, unobstructed in fact, and unready in every respect that readiness can be measured. No tolls will be paid, no infrastructure will assist, and no authority will either permit or forbid the transit, a combination of conditions the survey team describes as clarifying. The survey recommends continued monitoring of the corridor, which has exhibited no change during the observation period and is expected to exhibit none, a stability that makes monitoring both straightforward and, in the team's judgment, essential. A second survey will be scoped once the first has been fully absorbed by the planning office, which has confirmed receipt.
