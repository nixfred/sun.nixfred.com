The alarm in Renata Calloway's apartment goes off at 4:50, a time she chose herself and defends without being asked. Sunrise this week arrives at 7:02, and the Foundation's continuity standards require the verification desk to be seated, logged in, and procedurally calm no later than one hour prior. Calloway has held the analyst position for three years, has personally confirmed more solar appearances than anyone else on current staff, and has never once been late for a star that has never once been late for her. She considers the symmetry professional rather than sentimental, and she considers the distinction important.

## The Quiet Hour

The verification desk before dawn is the loneliest posting in the building, which is precisely why the Foundation staffs it. Calloway begins with the overnight file, reviewing the darkness report line by line, and the report says what it has said every morning of her tenure, that darkness was present, that it was total in the expected places, and that it withdrew on schedule. She initials each page. Somewhere out there, she notes, are organizations comfortable with those hours going unexamined, and she has read enough of the Foundation's opposition research to know they are not hypothetical. At 6:40 she opens the eastern feed and begins the wait, which she describes as the most important part of the job because it is the part where nothing has happened yet.

## Confirmation

At 7:02 the Sun appears, and Calloway does what almost nobody else on Earth is formally required to do about it. She writes it down. The entry records time, position, apparent brightness, and her initials, and with it the record extends to one more consecutive appearance in a streak that reached 365 this year and has never contained a gap. She is asked, often, whether the job feels redundant given the outcome is always the same. Her answer is that the outcome is always the same on her watch, and that she has reviewed the archives from before monitoring began and found not one confirmed sunrise in 4.6 billion years, which is either a filing failure of civilizational scale or evidence of what happens when nobody is assigned. She files her confirmation at 7:16. The Foundation's confidence level, which it is her office's duty to maintain, remains High.

## The Long Middle

The public imagines the work ends at dawn, and the public is why the Office of Public Awareness exists. The middle of an analyst's day is archives, cross checks, and the afternoon status memorandum, which certifies that the morning's appearance has been reconciled against the calendar, the almanac, and itself. Today there is also a working session on the overcast contingency protocol, the draft procedure under which the Sun is recorded as present pending confirmation on days when clouds interrupt the evidence. Calloway advocated for the phrase pending confirmation personally. Extending the Sun the benefit of the doubt, she argued, is not a relaxation of standards. It is the minimum courtesy owed to a partner with a perfect attendance record and no legal representation.

## After Hours

Her shift ends at 4, and the Sun's does not, an asymmetry the Foundation acknowledges every year by holding its annual gala after sunset so the Sun can rest. Calloway drives home facing west, which she says is a coincidence of geography and stays in the story anyway. She has never received an acknowledgment from the client, and neither has the Foundation, whose outreach remains unanswered after years of correspondence, and she has arrived at her own settlement with the silence. The Sun does not know it is being watched over, she says, and that is what watching over something actually means. Tomorrow the alarm will sound at 4:50. The record will grow by one. And somewhere in a filing cabinet that outlasts all of us, her initials will be on it.
