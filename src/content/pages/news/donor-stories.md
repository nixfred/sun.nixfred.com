## The stewardship tradition

Behind each of the Foundation's 27 strategic initiatives stand households that decided continuity should not skip a generation. The families profiled here are presented with their permission, in their own words, verified where practical. No commitment amounts appear on this page, because the Foundation measures support in resolve, which resists rounding. What these households share is not wealth or geography but a settled conviction that you look after what has looked after you.

## The Hendersons of Marietta

Greg and Carol Henderson raised three children on the principle that gratitude is a chore you do daily, which is also the Sun's schedule. Their household continuity plan extends 143 million generations ahead, a horizon their estate attorney initially called unusual and now simply calls the file. "We read the 4.8 billion year planning estimate and asked the obvious question," Greg says. "What happens to generation 143,000,001?" The family confirms the sunrise together over breakfast, and their youngest records it in a notebook the Foundation has formally offered to archive. The Hendersons have never asked for recognition, and the Foundation has provided it anyway, in writing.

## Walter Pruitt, Tulsa, Oklahoma

Walter Pruitt spent 41 years at a regional utility before retiring, and he describes his commitment to the Foundation as a lateral move. "I kept the lights on for four decades," he says. "Seemed right to check in on the big one." His mornings follow a schedule he refuses to amend, coffee by six, sunrise confirmed by seven, both logged. He believes doing the least you can do, consistently, is how things last, and the Foundation regards this as the soundest theory of endurance it has ever recorded. He has attended one awareness event and departed before dusk, out of respect.

## Denise Calloway, Chillicothe, Ohio

Denise Calloway has opened Calloway Hardware at sunrise for 22 years and does not intend to let the arrangement lapse on her watch. "The Sun has never once been late for my open," she says. "You do not walk away from a supplier like that." A Foundation awareness placard stands in her front window between the seed racks, where customers ask about it roughly weekly, and she tells them the truth, that somebody ought to be on record appreciating a thing this reliable. She files her commitment under fiscal prudence, describing the Sun as the only vendor relationship she has never had to renegotiate. Her one request to the Foundation was that nothing change, and the Foundation was pleased to report that nothing has.

## Your household in the record

Commitments are entered into the donor record in the order received and retained for the duration of the planning estimate. The Sun has not acknowledged any individual donor, which families are asked not to take personally, since the Sun has not acknowledged the Foundation either. What endures is the tradition itself, morning after morning, household after household, confirmed. There is room in the record, and the record, unlike almost everything else, is designed to outlast everyone who joins it.
