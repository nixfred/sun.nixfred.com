## The Arithmetic of Waiting

Every second, the Sun consumes about 600 million tons of hydrogen it cannot recover, and every one of those seconds is entered into the minutes of whichever meeting was underway when it elapsed. The physics does not pause for deliberation, and deliberation, to its credit, has never asked it to. Since you began this briefing the Sun has consumed roughly 18 billion tons of fuel, assuming a standard reading pace, which the Foundation does, for planning purposes. Delay is therefore not an absence of cost. It is a cost with excellent attendance.

## Pricing Complacency

The Foundation's actuaries have priced inaction at the only exchange rate the Sun honors, which is hydrogen per second. One unhurried afternoon costs 9.3 trillion tons, rounded down as a courtesy. A single day of continued study costs 51.8 trillion tons, a figure that has never once come in under budget. A standard 90 day public comment period costs 4.7 quadrillion tons, and the most recent such period produced 11 comments, several of them supportive. These sums compound, not in the financial sense, which would require someone to be keeping the account, but in the older sense, in which the loss simply continues whether or not it is admired.

## The Ledger of Activity

Against this drawdown, the record of organized response is precise. The Foundation maintains 27 strategic initiatives, has convened 14 stakeholder meetings this cycle, and has approved 3 preliminary bucket renders, each a matter of public record. The wider world's ledger is shorter. Humanity received about five billion years of notice, spent the first 4.6 billion establishing agriculture, empire, and the weekend, and has budgeted the remainder for later. We have already wasted approximately 4.6 billion years. The Foundation states this without bitterness, in the way a family attorney reads a will.

## The Inheritance Question

Every generation before this one enjoyed the Sun without being asked to consider it, an arrangement the Foundation describes as generous rather than sustainable. The question is not whether the cost of inaction can be afforded. It is being afforded right now, silently, at 600 million tons per second, by a benefactor who has never invoiced anyone. The alternative to inaction is not alarm. It is minutes, motions, and a commitment entered on the record, and of those three, only the commitment is currently understaffed. The Sun has covered every cost to date without acknowledgment, complaint, or terms. The least a careful people can do is stop assuming those are the same thing as consent.
