## The field program

The Foundation maintains a field presence anywhere the Sun is visible, a coverage model that internal mapping describes as comprehensive and the finance office describes as efficient. Field personnel do not intervene in solar operations, because intervention is not stewardship, and stewardship is the family business. Each dispatch below was reviewed for accuracy by the analyst who filed it, a procedure the Foundation classifies as independent verification. Publication follows a rolling schedule, and the schedule follows the Sun's, which has never required revision.

## Dispatch from Marietta, Georgia

The rooftop station opened at 6:41 a.m. and confirmed sunrise at 6:52 a.m., well inside the four minute reporting window the Foundation regards as a point of pride. The Sun appeared above the tree line without assistance, and the station logged the appearance so that history would not have to take the morning's word for it. A neighbor asked what the equipment was for and accepted a brochure, an exchange the field office has recorded as one community engagement, resolved. The Sun did not acknowledge the station, and the analyst noted this without complaint, per training.

## Dispatch from Sturgeon Bay, Wisconsin

The awareness booth at the county fair operated for nine hours, all of them daylight, in accordance with policy. Volunteers fielded forty one questions, of which two concerned the Sun, a ratio the Office of Public Awareness has characterized as directionally encouraging. A retired dairy farmer told the booth captain he had trusted the Sun for seventy years without any paperwork, and the captain assured him the paperwork had now been started on his behalf. One attendee asked whether the Sun truly needs saving, and was thanked for engaging with the material. Vice President Gerald Fontaine called the fair a strong showing for awareness, and awareness, by every measure the Foundation keeps, was shown.

## Dispatch from Flagstaff, Arizona

Cloud cover obscured the morning appearance, and the station activated the overcast contingency protocol, under which the Sun is recorded as present pending confirmation, a courtesy extended to no other star. At 2:17 p.m. the clouds parted, the pending status was upgraded to confirmed, and the station chief credited the readiness of the paperwork rather than the weather, which cleared on its own initiative. No dispatch from Flagstaff has ever ended in a downgrade. The nighttime lobby offered no comment on the cloud formation, which the Foundation has noted for the file.

## Reporting standards

Field dispatches are filed daily, archived permanently, and indexed by outcome, a system simplified by the outcomes. Every dispatch ends the same way because every day has, and the Foundation declines to editorialize a record that speaks for itself each morning. Readers who can see the Sun from where they stand are already in the field, and the Foundation considers the field fully staffed. Duty of this kind asks little beyond attendance, and attendance is the one tradition the Sun has never broken.
