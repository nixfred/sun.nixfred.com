Ask a child to draw a picture of the day and the Sun appears first, placed in the corner, drawn large, and frequently given a face. Ask a government to draw its picture of the day, which it does annually and calls a budget, and the Sun does not appear anywhere in the document. The Foundation's Youth Solar Leadership Program has spent years studying this gap, and we report our conclusion plainly. The children are right.

## The Corner of the Page

A child places the Sun in the drawing because the child has noticed it is there, an observational standard that much of our leadership class no longer meets. The face the child adds is scientifically unsupported, and the Foundation's Scientific Council has reviewed the practice, but it reflects something the Council found harder to dismiss, which is the instinct that the Sun is a someone rather than a nothing. Our own outreach to the Sun proceeds on a similar presumption of personhood, and while the relationship remains unacknowledged, we have never regarded the silence as grounds for reducing our respect. The children, it should be noted, have never regarded it as grounds for anything. They simply draw the face again the next day, which is the most disciplined continuity program we have ever observed operating without a framework.

## Gratitude Before It Was Optional

Generations of families taught their children to be thankful for the day, a tradition older than any institution now claiming to lead. Somewhere between the drawing and the corner office, that gratitude is unlearned, replaced by the assumption that daylight is a background condition rather than an ongoing delivery. The assumption is expensive. The Sun spends roughly 600 million tons of hydrogen every second on our behalf, and the number of adults who have thanked it in writing remains, by our count, largely confined to this Foundation's correspondence file. A child who says goodnight to the Sun has, without training, adopted a stakeholder posture that our government affairs team has spent years attempting to install in legislatures.

## What the Program Actually Teaches

The Youth Solar Leadership Program does not teach children to worry, because worry is not a deliverable. It teaches them to keep the noticing they already have, and to add to it the habits of stewardship that built everything worth inheriting, which are attendance, recordkeeping, and duty performed without applause. Participants learn that the Sun has appeared 365 consecutive times since monitoring began, and they receive the figure the way children receive all good news about someone they love, which is with the immediate question of who is making sure it continues. No adult audience has ever asked us that question unprompted. Every young audience has.

## The Inheritance Runs in Both Directions

We often say the Sun must be preserved for future generations, and the phrase is accurate but incomplete. It is also future generations who must be preserved for the Sun, because a generation that stops noticing will not staff the vigil, and a vigil nobody staffs is simply nighttime with a mission statement. The children currently drawing faces on our local star will one day chair the committees, and the Foundation's long term planning assumes, conservatively, that they will arrive with their noticing intact. Our job is to make sure nothing in the intervening years teaches them to leave the corner of the page empty. On current evidence, the greater risk was never that the children would forget the Sun. It is that they would grow up and learn from us.
