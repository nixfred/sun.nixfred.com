## A question we welcome

People ask why the Foundation concentrates its entire mission on one star when the observable universe contains billions. The question is fair, and the Foundation has answered it the way it answers everything, with a review. That review, conducted across fourteen stakeholder meetings, reached the conclusion the Sun had been quietly demonstrating for 4.6 billion years. Proximity is not sentiment. It is due diligence.

## The track record

The Sun has risen every day since records began, and the records began recently enough that we checked. Through wars, market corrections, and several ice ages, it has never missed a morning, a standard of attendance no other institution has matched and this one openly models itself on. Since formal monitoring commenced, 365 consecutive appearances have been confirmed, logged, and filed. Zero extinction events have occurred over the same period, a correlation our counsel permits us to note but not to claim.

## The agricultural relationship

Of the estimated 200 billion stars in this galaxy, exactly one maintains a verified working relationship with agriculture. Every crop ever harvested was grown under its supervision, without a contract, without an invoice, and without a single missed season. Distant stars photograph well, but not one of them has ripened a tomato, and not one has offered to. The Foundation believes you stand by the star that fed your grandparents, and their grandparents, back through an unbroken line of mornings.

## Loyalty as policy

Alternatives have been proposed, and the Foundation has studied each with the seriousness proposals deserve, which is a subcommittee. Proxima Centauri sits four light years away, a commute our analysts scored as unworkable for daylight purposes. Abandoning a proven star for an unproven one is exactly the kind of short term thinking the Foundation was retroactively established to prevent. The Sun has never once threatened to leave, a loyalty the nighttime lobby would prefer went unrewarded. It has also never acknowledged us, which the board has resolved to record as steadiness.

## Our answer

Why the Sun? Because it was here before us, because it shows up, and because it asks for nothing, which is precisely why an organization must ask on its behalf. Tradition of this kind is not chosen. It is inherited, and inheritance carries duty, and duty requires a framework, which we have. The Foundation serves 1 local star and intends to keep that number stable across all future planning periods. That is not a limitation of vision. It is focus, and focus is what 4.8 billion years of remaining runway demands.
