Priya Raghunathan serves as the Foundation's chief legal officer for matters beyond the atmosphere, a jurisdiction she notes is large, quiet, and thus far uncontested. She maintains the Foundation's standing under section 501(c)(nothing), a classification no authority has ever moved to revoke. She began her career in maritime law at Ellsworth Crane, drawn to the discipline as the nearest available precedent for a client that cannot come ashore. She later led the treaties practice at the Windermere Group, advising on agreements among parties who had never met, experience she describes as directly applicable. Her signature achievement at the Foundation is the standing memorandum on the Sun's legal position, now in its fourteenth revision, each ratified unanimously and none contested by opposing counsel, who has not appeared. She also secured the Foundation's rights in the phrase 1 local star served, a mark the star itself has declined to license. As a precaution she monitors the filings of lunar interests, noting that the Moon has retained no counsel, which is exactly what she would advise it to say. The first lawyer in a family of engineers, she was raised on the principle that a thing properly maintained never needs to be argued about.
