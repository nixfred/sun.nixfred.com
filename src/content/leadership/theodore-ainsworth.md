Theodore Ainsworth is responsible for ensuring that the Foundation's plans extend at least as far as the problem they address. His office maintains the strategic calendar through the year 4,800,002,026, believed to be the only planning document of its length still current. He joined the Foundation from the Cascadia Actuarial Group, where he priced century bonds and found the durations restless. Earlier he served as deputy director of the Institute for Generational Planning, whose fifty year strategic plan he completed drafting in eleven months, a pace the Institute's board asked him never to repeat. His signature achievement at the Foundation is the Long Horizon Review, a rolling assessment that has confirmed in each of the 27 strategic initiatives a horizon judged sufficiently long. He championed the 4.8 billion year planning estimate over objections that it was alarmist, and over separate objections that it was generous, a spread he cites as evidence of calibration. He often observes that the Sun has kept the same schedule for 4.6 billion years without a single published revision, a discipline his office aspires to and, in the meantime, documents. Mr. Ainsworth credits his grandfather, a watchmaker, with the family principle that nothing worth keeping runs fast.
