Marisol Vega oversees the office charged with the eventual replenishment of solar hydrogen, an assignment she accepted on the condition that it be treated as logistics. The Sun consumes roughly 600 million tons of hydrogen every second, a figure her department classifies as a scheduling constraint rather than a discouragement. She spent nineteen years at Calloway Bulk Carriers, rising from night dispatcher to Vice President of Continental Freight, and never lost a shipment she was told about. Her signature achievement at the Foundation is board approval of 3 preliminary bucket renders, the replenishment program's most tangible deliverable to date and the subject of a forthcoming handle stress testing schedule. Under her direction the office has completed its survey of the 93 million mile primary route, finding no tolls but limited infrastructure. She is unhurried about the pace of the work, noting that the hydrogen situation has not yet responded to the strategic framework and that her office has budgeted for that patience. Colleagues describe her as the rare executive who reads every appendix, including the ones she commissions to check the others. A rancher's daughter, she keeps her father's advice framed above her desk: measure twice, haul once.
