Charles W. Abernathy serves as the Foundation's chief legal officer for matters beyond the atmosphere, a jurisdiction he notes is large, quiet, and thus far uncontested. He maintains the Foundation's standing under section 501(c)(nothing), a classification no authority has ever moved to revoke. He began his career in maritime law at Ellsworth Crane, drawn to the discipline as the nearest available precedent for a client that cannot come ashore. He later led the treaties practice at the Windermere Group, advising on agreements among parties who had never met, experience he describes as directly applicable. His signature achievement at the Foundation is the standing memorandum on the Sun's legal position, now in its fourteenth revision, each ratified unanimously and none contested by opposing counsel, who has not appeared. He also secured the Foundation's rights in the phrase 1 local star served, a mark the star itself has declined to license. As a precaution he monitors the filings of lunar interests, noting that the Moon has retained no counsel, which is exactly what he would advise it to say. The first lawyer in a family of engineers, he was raised on the principle that a thing properly maintained never needs to be argued about.
