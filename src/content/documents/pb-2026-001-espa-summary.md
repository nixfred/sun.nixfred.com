## 1. Status of the Model Bill

The Emergency Stellar Preservation Act (the Act) is model legislation prepared by the Solar Save Foundation for adoption by any legislature prepared to meet the moment. The Act has not been introduced in any chamber, a circumstance the Foundation attributes to the legislative calendar rather than to the merits. Drafting concluded approximately 4.6 billion years after the emergency began, which the drafting committee regards as prompt under the circumstances. General Counsel Charles W. Abernathy has certified the text as ready for introduction, and readiness is a standard this office does not certify lightly.

## 2. Definitions (Act Section 2)

The Act opens with definitions, because a crisis of this magnitude cannot afford ambiguity about what is being preserved.

| Defined term | Meaning under the Act |
| --- | --- |
| The Sun | The sole star currently serving the jurisdiction, in continuous operation since approximately 4,600,000,000 BC. |
| Daylight | The Sun's principal deliverable, provided each day without invoice. |
| Continuity | The condition in which the Sun continues, as confirmed by observation each morning. |
| Extinction event | Any morning on which the foregoing definitions fail. Recorded instances to date, zero. |

The committee debated whether the Sun required a statutory definition at all, and concluded that a star which has never acknowledged the relationship should at minimum be identified in writing.

## 3. The Bipartisan Solar Continuity Commission (Act Sections 3 through 5)

The centerpiece of the Act is the establishment of a Bipartisan Solar Continuity Commission, drawn equally from both parties, because daylight falls on each without preference. The Commission is directed to convene quarterly, during daylight hours as a matter of principle, and to develop a national framework for solar continuity. It is granted no enforcement powers, no staff, and no deadline, a structure the drafters selected to insulate it from the pressures that compromise more active bodies. Its first statutory obligation is an organizational meeting, and the drafting notes observe that the Foundation's own 14 stakeholder meetings supply a proven template for what such a meeting can accomplish.

## 4. Reporting Requirements (Act Sections 6 and 7)

Section 6 requires the Commission to submit an annual report confirming whether the Sun appeared during the reporting period. Section 7 requires a biennial review of the annual reports, so that the record receives the same vigilance as the star. The drafting notes cite the Foundation's 365 consecutive confirmed solar appearances as the only body of precedent in the field, and as proof that reporting of this kind is achievable at scale. Nothing in either section directs any party to alter the Sun's behavior, which the drafters considered beyond the police powers of any state.

## 5. Authorization of Appropriations (Act Section 8)

Section 8 authorizes no appropriations. The drafters concluded that a preservation effort of this magnitude should not begin by handling money, a discipline the Foundation has maintained since founding with results the sector has yet to match. The section instead authorizes such coordination, awareness, and alignment as may be necessary, all of which cost nothing and are therefore fully funded upon enactment.

## 6. The Sunset Clause (Act Section 9)

Section 9 provides that the Act expires five years after enactment unless renewed. The drafting committee acknowledges that the customary name for this provision is unfortunate, and the record should reflect that the term was inherited, not chosen. Counsel reviewed the available alternatives, including expiration clause and terminal provision, and found each worse. The clause accordingly stands under its traditional name, accompanied by a drafting note stating that no expiration of the Act shall be construed as acceptance of any other expiration, and that renewal dates have been calendared through the full 4.8 billion year planning horizon in anticipation of sustained legislative interest.

## 7. Concluding Assessment

The Act is complete, reviewed twice, and awaiting only a sponsor, a chamber, and a vote. Until those materialize, the Foundation will maintain the text in a state of readiness, amending the definitions should the Sun's status change, which monitoring confirms it has not. The bill accomplishes nothing until enacted, and the Foundation notes for the record that in this respect it is already performing exactly as drafted.
