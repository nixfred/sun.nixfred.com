## Executive summary

No hearing has ever been convened on the projected death of the Sun. This brief exists to make that sentence impossible to read comfortably. The Emergency Stellar Preservation Act, drafted to 340 pages, would make solar continuity a matter of federal record for the first time in the history of any government. The Act imposes no costs, creates no agency, and mandates no action, because the Foundation's legislative philosophy holds that recognition must precede appropriation, ideally by decades. The Sun has risen over all fifty states every day since before there were states, an attendance record no federal program has matched and no federal document has acknowledged. This brief has been prepared for members of both parties, since the Sun shines on each with a neutrality the Foundation considers instructive. It closes with a finding offered to both equally. Complacency is currently the only solar policy of the United States, and it is the one policy that has never lacked support.

## Key findings

1. Congressional neglect of the Sun is fully bipartisan, with every member of both chambers scoring an Incomplete on the Foundation's legislative scorecard for the fourth consecutive cycle.
2. The Act has been referred to committee, which this brief records as the first official contact between the United States government and the solar continuity question.
3. Federal disaster frameworks currently address floods, fires, and hurricanes, all of which are survivable, while the one event that is not survivable appears in no framework at all.
4. Constituent outreach templates have been distributed nationally, and early participants report their representatives responded within 3 to 5 business days with letters confirming receipt.
5. The nighttime lobby maintains a registered presence in the capital while the Sun, which cannot register, is represented only by the Foundation, an asymmetry this brief identifies as the oldest special interest imbalance on record.
