Gerald Fontaine leads the Foundation's public awareness portfolio, where the numbers describe an institution at the height of its influence. Under his direction, awareness of the Sun stands at 100 percent in every market surveyed, a result his office verified twice at its own insistence. He came to the role after fourteen years at Harding Mercer Public Affairs, where he managed reputational continuity for clients who preferred not to be named and, in most cases, still do. Earlier he directed communications for the National Trust for Familiar Landmarks, which he calls formative preparation for representing the most familiar landmark of all. His signature achievement at the Foundation is the modern gala program, held after sunset each year so the Sun can rest, an accommodation the Sun has never acknowledged and Mr. Fontaine has never mentioned. His office releases each morning's solar appearance announcement to the public at no charge, a practice he regards as the least the Foundation can do, and among the most it does. He also chairs the internal committee that reviews what the nighttime lobby is telling the public, and more importantly, when. He and his wife raise their children on the principle that showing up daily is the whole of character, citing a familiar example.
