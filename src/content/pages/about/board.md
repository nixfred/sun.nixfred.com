## Composition

The Board of Trustees is the Foundation's senior governing body, entrusted with protecting 4.6 billion years of solar tradition through quarterly meetings. Eleven voting trustees serve alongside one nonvoting seat, an arrangement the bylaws describe as balanced. The board convened for 14 stakeholder meetings this cycle, and each concluded with a resolution to meet again. Trustees serve without compensation, a sacrifice the Treasurer confirms has cost the Foundation nothing.

## Officers of the Board

1. Chair. The Honorable Eleanor V. Prescott (Ret.) has chaired the board since founding, presiding over every unanimous vote in the institution's history. She is credited with the board's signature achievement, the strategic framework process, which has produced 27 strategic initiatives and is expected to produce more.
2. Vice Chair. Ambassador Willard T. Kensington III supports the Chair and stands ready to preside should the Chair be unavailable, a contingency for which three preliminary protocols have been drafted and one approved.
3. Treasurer. Beatrice A. Thornbury, CPA, stewards a balance sheet of exemplary simplicity and has closed every fiscal year without a single dollar unaccounted for. Her annual report is regarded across the sector as the only perfect document of its kind.
4. Secretary. Colonel Edmund R. Fairleigh (Ret.) keeps the minutes, which are complete, initialed, and now considerably longer than the outcomes they record.

## Independent Trustees

Six independent trustees provide outside perspective, each selected for distinguished service to institutions and for holding no interest in a competing star. Their independence is reviewed annually and has been confirmed every year, unanimously, by the people it describes. Collectively they bring experience in stewardship, fiscal prudence, and legacy planning across a combined 4.8 billion year horizon, which is the organizational planning estimate and not, the board wishes to clarify, a personal one.

## The Youth Observer

The Youth Observer seat ensures the next generation has a voice in solar continuity. It has been held since 1989 by Mr. Tanner W. Ashbrook, whose status as a youth was affirmed by board resolution and is not subject to review. He attends all meetings, may speak when recognized, and will assume voting rights at an age the bylaws describe as forthcoming.

## The Nonvoting Solar Representative

One seat on the board is reserved in perpetuity for the Sun. The position has been vacant since founding, and outreach continues. A chair is set at every meeting, facing east, and the agenda is read aloud in its direction as a courtesy. The Sun has never acknowledged the invitation, the seat, or the relationship, a silence the board has voted, unanimously, to interpret as trust.
